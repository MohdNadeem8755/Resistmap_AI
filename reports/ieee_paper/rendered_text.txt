Abstract—Genomic prediction of antimicrobial resistance can
support surveillance, but shared sampling projects and repeated
resistance profiles complicate evaluation. ResistMap AI combines
auditable genomic feature encoding, project-separated model
development, and a sequence-processing workflow for Klebsiella
pneumoniae. We evaluate binary ceftazidime resistance using
public susceptibility records with an explicit CLSI testing-
standard annotation and matched resistance genotypes. The
saved cohort contains 849 isolates; overlap filtering retains
445 development isolates and 114 historical test isolates, while
excluding 290 overlapping candidates. Complete gene calls and
reported point mutations are encoded as binary predictors. Three
development folds separate BioProjects and remove overlapping
feature profiles and known SNP clusters from training. A random
forest selected jointly with its decision threshold achieves mean
fold accuracy of 90.37%, balanced accuracy of 83.66%, and
resistant-class recall of 93.98%. Balanced accuracy ranges from
67.15% to 97.42% across folds, exposing substantial heterogene-
ity. A separate ten-genome sequence pilot establishes processing
feasibility without measuring predictive accuracy. These develop-
ment results support further evaluation of the framework, but do
not establish prospective diagnostic performance or independent
validation of the selected model.
Index Terms—antimicrobial resistance, Klebsiella pneumoniae,
genomic surveillance, machine learning, ceftazidime
I. INTRODUCTION
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) complicates the treatment
of bacterial infections. The global analysis of resistance burden
in 2019 identifiedKlebsiella pneumoniaeamong the pathogens
responsible for the largest numbers of resistance-associated
deaths [1]. Genomic surveillance offers a means to charac-
terize resistance determinants alongside bacterial population
structure. Tools such as Kleborate already provide species-
specific genotyping infrastructure [2]; prediction models add a
different task: estimating a measured susceptibility phenotype
from genomic evidence.
Earlier work demonstrated that whole-genome features can
predict minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for mul-
tiple antibiotics inKlebsiella pneumoniae[3]. However, a
useful prediction score depends on what was withheld during
evaluation. Isolates from the same study may share laboratory
practices, lineages, or resistance profiles. Separating rows at
random does not necessarily separate these sources of depen-
dence. More broadly, leakage can make reported machine-
learning performance exceed the evidence supported by the
evaluation design [4].
ResistMap AI addresses this evaluation problem through an
auditable workflow rather than a new classification algorithm.
The present study evaluates ceftazidime as a defined species–
drug task. Its contributions are: (1) a binary gene/mutation rep-
resentation linked to a saved isolate manifest; (2) a BioProject-
separated development protocol with additional profile and
known-cluster overlap controls, reporting class-specific per-
formance; and (3) a sequence-processing branch that preserves
provenance and quality flags separately from model predictors.
The third contribution is evaluated for technical feasibility, not
predictive gain.
Section II positions the framework against resistance anno-
tation and prediction methods. Section III defines its process-
ing and learning steps. Section IV describes the cohort and
evaluation protocol. Section V reports the saved experimental
results and their limitations. Section VI identifies the next
validation step.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Resistance Annotation and Surveillance
AMRFinderPlus identifies resistance genes and selected
resistance-associated mutations, with additional categories
covering stress responses and virulence [5]. ResFinder 4.0 con-
nects resistance genotypes to phenotype predictions through
curated interpretation [6]. CARD supplies curated resistance
determinants and tools for resistome analysis [7]. These re-
sources support feature construction, but a detected determi-
nant and a measured susceptibility category remain different
forms of evidence. ResistMap AI learns their association for
one drug using recorded susceptibility labels.
Database curation also matters: changes in reference content
and interpretation can change reported features [8]. Accord-
ingly, the sequence branch records the software and database
versions used for annotation. Kleborate supports species and
assembly screening [2]. The framework uses these checks to

identify records requiring review; a species match is not treated
as proof that an assembly is free of contamination.
B. Genotype-to-Phenotype Prediction
Nguyenet al.trained a genome-based MIC predictor using
1,668Klebsiella pneumoniaeisolates and 20 antibiotics [3].
That multi-drug MIC endpoint differs from the binary cef-
tazidime endpoint considered here. For polymyxins, Macesic
et al.investigated curated genomic predictors in clonal group
258 and showed that feature construction affects predictive
performance [9]. Batisti Biffignandiet al.examined how MIC
representation and model choice influence prediction [10].
These studies motivate explicit endpoint and feature definitions
rather than treating all AMR prediction tasks as interchange-
able.
A larger study by Jiaet al.evaluated multi-level resis-
tance prediction using 5,239 genomes and 11 antibiotics [11].
ResistMap AI does not claim better performance than these
studies: cohort composition, susceptibility interpretation, end-
point, and validation design differ. Its narrower contribution
is a traceable development analysis that makes overlap ex-
clusions and between-fold failures visible. The distinction
between model selection and independent evaluation fol-
lows the methodological concerns identified by Kapoor and
Narayanan [4].
III. PROPOSEDMETHOD
A. Cohort Assembly and Feature Encoding
The workflow begins with local exports of public NCBI
susceptibility and isolate metadata. BioSample accessions con-
nect the two tables. Species names are restricted toKlebsiella
pneumoniae, allowing strain or subspecies suffixes. Antibi-
otic and phenotype strings are normalized before matching.
For each BioSample–antibiotic pair, conflicting susceptible,
intermediate, or resistant records are identified before inter-
mediate records are removed. The binary cohort requires an
unambiguous susceptible or resistant label, available genotype
information, and a ceftazidime record explicitly annotated with
the CLSI testing standard. This criterion uses the deposited
categorical label; it does not reclassify MICs using a newly
chosen breakpoint edition.
For isolatei, the feature dictionary contains binary indica-
tors for genotype tokens markedCOMPLETEorPOINT. Gene
and mutation keys use separate namespaces. For a retained

B. Partitioning and Overlap Control
The outer partition uses BioProject groups. Candidate splits
are generated with a fixed seed of 42 and a nominal test-group
fraction of 0.20. Before acceptance, training candidates sharing
an identical encoded profile or a known SNP-cluster accession
with the test partition are removed. Accession versions are
ignored for SNP-cluster comparison. The first split satisfying
at least 400 retained training isolates, 100–250 test isolates,
at least 50 training isolates per class, at least 20 test isolates
per class, and at least five training projects is retained. These
criteria use sample counts and labels, not model scores.
Within the development partition, three stratified group folds
use BioProject as the grouping variable. Training candidates
overlapping a validation profile or known SNP cluster are
again removed. Each development isolate receives one out-
of-fold prediction. Missing cluster identifiers cannot establish
unrelatedness; consequently, these checks reduce measured
overlap without guaranteeing full phylogenetic independence.

boosting, and a majority-class reference. The selected random
forest contains 600 trees, a minimum leaf size of four, balanced
class weights, and a feature-sampling fraction of 0.5. Its
probability output is the mean resistant-class estimate across
trees. The decision rule is

E. Workflow Boundaries and Evidence Flow
Fig. 1 separates the data used for prediction from the data
retained for auditing. The distinction prevents an adminis-
trative field from becoming a shortcut for the phenotype.
BioProject identifies the sampling group during partitioning; it
is never passed to the classifier. Similarly, susceptibility labels
determine the target and permit stratification, but neither the
categorical label nor its underlying MIC enters the feature
dictionary. Source type and assembly-quality flags describe
measurement conditions and remain available for review with-
out becoming predictive inputs.
The trained representation and the sequence-regeneration
branch have different provenance. The former consumes the
genotype strings in an existing public export. The latter
regenerates evidence from downloaded nucleotide assemblies
or read sets using a recorded tool environment. A shared
gene name does not establish equivalence between the two
representations: reference versions, alignment rules, partial-
call handling, and mutation coverage may differ. A transition
to regenerated features therefore requires a new feature audit
and model evaluation, even if the interface and target antibiotic
stay the same.
An isolate record has three conceptual parts: identifiers
for joining and auditing, measured or deposited evidence,
and model-derived output. Maintaining these parts separately
permits an analyst to trace an error back to its original
genotype and susceptibility record. It also prevents a model
probability from being mistaken for an experimental suscep-
tibility measurement. The current manuscript evaluates the
classification component of this workflow; it does not assume
that a dashboard display supplies additional validation.
F . Assumptions Behind the Binary Representation
The representation assumes that reported complete genes
and known point mutations contain useful information about
the deposited ceftazidime phenotype. It does not assume that
every encoded determinant is specific to ceftazidime. The
model may use co-occurring determinants as statistical mark-
ers, including features whose association reflects the sampled
bacterial population rather than a direct resistance mechanism.
Accordingly, a high predictive contribution would require
separate biological investigation before being described as
causal.
Repeated occurrences of the same retained token within
an isolate produce a single indicator. This deliberately avoids
interpreting duplicated annotations as gene dosage. Gene and
mutation namespaces remain distinct, so a gene call cannot
silently replace a point-mutation call with the same text.
Tokens with excluded statuses do not contribute to the primary
vector, although their raw source record remains relevant when
reviewing an error. Malformed entries are treated as parsing
problems rather than silently converted into a susceptible label.
A training-fold vocabulary defines the columns available to
a fitted model. A validation isolate can contain a determinant
absent from that vocabulary; the model cannot learn an effect
for that determinant from the current training fold. This is an
information limitation, not a reason to rebuild the vocabulary
using validation rows. The appropriate response is to retain
the unseen-feature evidence for audit and evaluate the model
under the same information constraint that applies to a new
isolate.
G. Why Two Levels of Separation Are Needed
The outer partition and the development folds serve different
purposes. The outer split originally created the historical V1
test, while the inner folds support model comparison on the re-
tained development set. Project separation at both levels blocks
direct reuse of a project’s isolates across the corresponding fit
and evaluation partitions. Profile and known-cluster filtering
address a different problem: distinct project accessions can
still contain closely related or identically represented isolates.
Filtering acts on training candidates, leaving the evaluation
partition intact. Reversing that direction would change the
evaluation population in response to the available training
data. The resulting training set can become smaller than the
nominal group split suggests; that loss is part of the protocol
and must be retained in the manifest. A model fitted after
overlap removal therefore faces a different information budget
from a model evaluated by a conventional random row split.
The procedure uses observed annotations rather than a full
reconstruction of evolutionary relationships. Identical feature
profiles are a conservative computational overlap criterion,
not proof that two isolates are identical genomes. Conversely,
different profiles do not prove that two isolates are unrelated.
A known SNP-cluster identifier supplies additional evidence,
but an absent identifier leaves uncertainty. These distinctions
explain why the manuscript describes the protocol as project-

H. Recorded Selection Procedure
For each candidate, three fitted pipelines produce one prob-
ability for every development isolate in its assigned validation
fold. The 17 thresholds are applied to these saved probabilities;
changing the threshold does not require retraining the candi-
date. Thus, V2 uses 33 cross-validation fits, followed by one
final development-set fit, while comparing 187 configuration–
threshold combinations. This count describes the V2 stage only
and excludes earlier V1 tuning and feature experiments.
The saved decision rule uses a greater-than-or-equal com-
parison. An output exactly equal to 0.40 is therefore classified
as resistant. This small implementation detail matters when
reproducing classifications from rounded probabilities. The
archived floating-point values, rather than the rounded values
printed in a table, should be used to recreate decisions. The
selected threshold must also remain attached to the trained
pipeline: retaining only the forest and reverting to a default
decision boundary would reproduce a different predictor.
The mean fold objective, worst-fold tie breaker, and thresh-
old tie breaker are recorded explicitly. No project-specific
threshold is fitted. Nevertheless, searching the same folds
over candidate models and thresholds is an adaptive use of
those folds. The procedure supports a transparent development
choice; it does not convert the winning cross-validation score
into a final generalization estimate.

The remaining 290 records were excluded from the outer

The manuscript audit checks that the 445 selected-model
probability rows have unique BioSample accessions and ex-
actly match the manifest’s development identifiers. It re-
constructs the thresholded confusion counts for each fold
and checks their balanced accuracies against the saved fold-

The same distinction applies to software provenance. The
recorded library versions describe the saved training environ-
ment; they are not a claim that every future version will re-
produce identical results. The final model, feature vocabulary,
threshold, candidate configuration, and data partition need to
be preserved together. Re-running only the estimator with a
different export or library environment would constitute a new
experiment. File checksums recorded during the manuscript
audit identify the local result files used for this draft.
E. Comparability of Development Experiments
All rows in the initial baseline comparison use the same
saved development folds and primary COMPLETE/POINT
feature representation. The majority classifier provides a refer-
ence for the observed class skew; the logistic model supplies
a linear reference; the two ensemble families allow nonlinear
combinations of binary features. These are within-cohort com-
parisons. Their ordering does not imply that one model family
is superior for other drugs, species, or sampling distributions.
The V1 control and selected V2 row differ from untuned
baselines because their settings were chosen after development
comparisons. Reporting them together is useful for document-
ing the development trajectory, provided that the selection
status is explicit. It would be misleading to interpret every
difference in the table as if it arose from a single prespecified
experiment. Earlier feature exploration and the historical test
also constrain the independence that can be claimed for the
final choice.
The partial-call experiment is a restricted representation
comparison rather than a new sequence experiment. It adds
information from alternative statuses in the existing export
while retaining the same endpoint and saved folds. It does
not measure the effect of replacing the export with a uni-
formly re-annotated genome cohort. That larger change would
require completing the separate processing branch, assessing
missingness and quality flags, and repeating the overlap audit
for its resulting feature representation.

V. RESULTS ANDDISCUSSION
A. Development Performance
Table II and Fig. 3 compare the saved development results.
The selected V2 model reaches mean accuracy of 90.37%
and BA of 83.66%. These values answer different questions:
accuracy weights isolates equally, while BA weights the two
classes equally. Majority prediction already reaches 81.54%
mean accuracy but has zero susceptible recall. Accuracy alone
would conceal that failure.
Relative to the V1 control at threshold 0.50, V2 increases
mean accuracy by 2.26 percentage points and BA by 0.57
points. Resistant recall rises from 90.68% to 93.98%, while
susceptible recall falls from 75.51% to 73.35%. Both tree
configuration and threshold change, so this comparison cannot
attribute the difference to threshold adjustment alone. Nor does
the small BA difference demonstrate a statistically established
improvement.

D. Limitations and Surveillance Interpretation
The cohort is small, resistance-enriched, and assembled
from heterogeneous public projects. Requiring available geno-
type calls excludes records without such results. Removing
290 overlapping candidates changes the evaluated population
and limits comparison with unrestricted random splits. BioPro-
ject and known-cluster separation cannot rule out unrecorded
shared ancestry. Deposited CLSI labels also do not establish
a uniform testing method or breakpoint edition across labora-
tories.
The feature representation captures reported genes and
point mutations, but does not explicitly quantify gene ex-
pression, copy number, or all regulatory and structural vari-
ation. Database versions may change feature calls. Repeated
development-set comparisons create selection optimism even
when preprocessing is fitted within each fold. Finally, the
present experiment evaluates isolate-level classification; it
does not measure outbreak detection, alert lead time, geo-
graphic forecasting, treatment benefit, or prospective clinical
turnaround time. The framework can organize evidence for
surveillance review, but those operational outcomes require
separate validation.

E. Threshold Sensitivity Within the Selected Configuration
Table IV holds the RF leaf 4 candidate fixed and varies
only the decision threshold using its saved out-of-fold prob-
abilities. This comparison isolates a decision-rule effect that
the V1–V2 comparison cannot isolate. Moving from 0.50 to
0.40 raises mean accuracy from 85.96% to 90.37% and BA
from 81.58% to 83.66%. Resistant recall increases by 5.45
percentage points, whereas susceptible recall decreases by 1.28
points.
Reducing the threshold further to 0.30 gives slightly lower
accuracy than 0.40 but a much larger reduction in susceptible
recall. Raising it to 0.60 recovers little susceptible recall while
losing resistant recall. This pattern explains why the recorded
development objective selects 0.40 from the grid. It does not
establish that 0.40 is optimal for a new hospital, laboratory,
or prevalence setting.
The best mean BA and best worst-fold BA are also not
identical objectives. Among the displayed thresholds, 0.50 has
a higher worst-fold BA than 0.40 even though its mean BA
is lower. The implemented tie breaker cannot override a dif-
ference in the primary mean objective. If a future application
prioritizes minimum subgroup performance, that requirement
should be stated before model selection and evaluated inde-
pendently, rather than introduced after inspecting a difficult
fold.

G. Interpretation of Regenerated Sequence Evidence
The processing checkpoint supports a limited but useful
conclusion: the workflow can connect public accessions to
nucleotide assemblies and reproducible annotation outputs for
a substantial part of the development cohort. It cannot yet
answer whether regenerated evidence improves classification.
The completed assembly subset and remaining read-derived
subset have different data-availability conditions. Training
only on the convenient completed subset could change the
cohort and make a feature comparison inseparable from a
sampling comparison.
Completeness should therefore be evaluated at the manifest
level. An isolate with a failed download is different from
an isolate with an assembly warning, and both differ from
an isolate with a valid annotation containing no retained
resistance feature. Treating all three as an all-zero vector
would hide distinct evidence states. The saved processing
records support keeping these states separate while unresolved

cases are reviewed. No unresolved sample should be removed
because its current model prediction is wrong.
The mapping checks also require contextual interpretation.
Aligning reads back to their own assembly tests whether
assembled sequence is supported by those reads, but missing
sequence can remain absent from both the reference and
the evaluated breadth denominator. Similarly, a close species
match is compatible with additional contamination that a ded-
icated screen might detect. The retained uniform-quality flag
demonstrates why quality summaries should not be reduced to
a single pass/fail score. These are reasons to retain raw reports,
not evidence that a specific isolate is biologically invalid.
H. Requirements for the Next Evaluation
The next evaluation should distinguish a feature-
development stage from a final validation stage. First, the
complete sequence-derived representation should be defined,
including partial calls, mutation states, missingness, database
version, and quality-review rules. Candidate comparison
can then proceed using development data with the overlap
restrictions applied to that representation. Only after the
pipeline and threshold are frozen should an independent
cohort be used to estimate generalization.
The independent cohort should document the phenotype
testing method and interpretive standard, retain accession
provenance, and include enough susceptible and resistant
isolates to interpret both error denominators. Evaluation should
report accuracy, BA, class recalls, ranking performance, and
confusion counts under the frozen decision rule. Any calibra-
tion assessment or alternative operating threshold should be
declared as a separate analysis. These are requirements for a
future study; they are not completed experiments in the present
paper.
Operational surveillance would require an additional eval-
uation beyond isolate classification. For example, measuring
whether flagged isolates support earlier investigation requires
timestamps, a reference definition of an event, and an explicit
alert policy. None of those outcomes is available in the
present saved experiment. The immediate result is therefore
a reproducible prediction-development record and a sequence-
processing feasibility demonstration. Keeping that boundary
explicit makes later claims testable when operational data
become available.
VI. CONCLUSION
ResistMap AI provides an auditable workflow for linking
genomic resistance calls to ceftazidime susceptibility inKleb-
siella pneumoniae. On three project-separated development
folds, the selected random forest achieved 90.37% mean accu-
racy and 83.66% balanced accuracy. The 67.15–97.42% fold-
level BA range demonstrates that the average does not describe
uniform performance across held-out projects. Sequence pro-
cessing was feasible in a ten-genome pilot, but the regenerated
features have not yet been evaluated as a trained predictor. The
next study should freeze the complete feature pipeline, model,
and threshold before testing on a newly acquired, independent
cohort with documented phenotype methods and sequence
quality.
